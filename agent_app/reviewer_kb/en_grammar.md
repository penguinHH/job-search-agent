# English grammar & style checklist (job applications)

## Grammar
- Subject–verb agreement, especially with long subjects ("The results of the benchmark *show*…").
- Articles: a/an/the before singular countable nouns ("I built *a* benchmark environment", "*the* B200 GPU"); no article before uncountable/plural generic nouns ("experience in machine learning"). Typical errors of Chinese/Japanese speakers: missing articles, "an experience" (uncountable sense), "informations", "researches" (as a noun meaning studies → "research").
- Tense: past simple for finished work ("I developed"), present perfect for experience up to now ("I have worked with Docker since 2023"), present for current roles. Keep tense consistent inside one bullet list.
- Prepositions: "experience *in/with* X", "interested *in*", "graduate *from*", "responsible *for*", "at the University of Tokyo", "in 2027", "on 6 October".
- Countable vs uncountable: equipment, software, feedback, advice, research, knowledge → no plural -s.
- Parallel structure in lists ("designing, implementing and testing", not "design, implementing and tested").
- Avoid dangling modifiers ("Using PyTorch, the model was trained" → "Using PyTorch, I trained the model").
- Hyphenation of compound adjectives before nouns: "first-author paper", "long-term internship", "real-time tracking".

## Style
- Active voice and strong verbs: built, led, designed, reduced, automated, benchmarked. Avoid "was responsible for" and "helped with" when a concrete verb exists.
- Concise: remove fillers ("very", "really", "in order to" → "to", "a number of" → "several"/the number).
- One idea per sentence; sentences under ~25 words in emails and cover letters.
- British vs American spelling: pick one and keep it (optimise/optimize).
- Numbers: spell out one to nine in prose, digits for 10+ and for all metrics/units ("7×", "95%", "3 years" is fine in CVs).
- Dates: unambiguous ("6 October 2026" or "October 6, 2026"), never "10/6".
- Tone: confident but not boastful; no "I am the best candidate"; no exclamation marks in formal mail.
- Do not translate Japanese politeness formulas literally ("Thank you for your continued support" is fine; "I humbly ask" is not).

## Email conventions
- Subject: specific ("Application: Applied Research Engineer – Xu Mingge").
- Salutation: "Dear Ms./Mr. Surname" or "Dear Hiring Team"; "Hi Name," only if they wrote informally first.
- Closing: "Best regards," / "Kind regards," + full name + affiliation + contact.
- Reply-all when the sender asks for it or when CC'd colleagues need to stay informed.
