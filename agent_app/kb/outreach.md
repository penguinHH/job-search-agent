# Writing to companies (outreach rules)

Shared by the GUI agent and Claude Code sessions. Candidate-specific rules (start date, what may not be
disclosed about the current employer, reusable strengths) live in the personal knowledge file
(`资料/knowledge.md` → "Candidate-specific writing rules"); the writing reviewer additionally checks grammar,
keigo and document conventions (`agent_app/reviewer_kb/`).

## Ground rules

- Facts about the candidate come only from the profile / materials. Missing → visible placeholder
  (【要記入】 / [TO FILL]) and ask the user. Never invent experience, numbers, dates or publications.
- Facts about the company must be ones you verified (site, job posting, news). Mentioning a specific
  product, paper, case study or posting is what makes the message worth reading — generic praise is not.
- Use the official route first: the ATS posting (HRMOS / HERP / Talentio / Greenhouse / Lever / Ashby …)
  or the company's stated contact. Cold e-mail only to addresses the company itself publishes.
  Casual-interview (カジュアル面談) requests are normal in Japanese startups and a good low-friction first step.
- Be upfront about timing and status (start date, student status, part-time limits) as stated in the
  personal knowledge file. Visa sponsorship: don't lead with it in a first cold e-mail to a Japanese company,
  but mention the required change of residence status briefly and positively; raise it clearly before or at
  the first interview. Foreign-oriented companies can be told up front.
- Never disclose internal or confidential details of a current or past employer beyond what the CV states
  (internal wikis, server paths, customer names under NDA, unreleased products).
- Outward actions are gated: e-mails go to the approval queue, form submissions wait for the user's approval,
  CAPTCHAs / logins / passwords are always left to the user.

## Choosing the language

- Japanese company, Japanese careers page → Japanese (ビジネス敬語, 書き言葉では「貴社」).
- English careers page / English-first company → English.
- Bilingual postings → match the posting the user applies to; an English message with a short Japanese
  greeting is fine for international teams.
- Chinese only if the counterpart is clearly Chinese-speaking and the user asks.

## Cold e-mail / casual-interview request (Japanese)

Body ~300–450 characters (応募メール up to ~800). Structure:

```
件名: 【カジュアル面談のお願い】<職種名> / <大学・課程> <氏名>

<会社名>
採用ご担当者様

突然のご連絡失礼いたします。<所属（大学院・研究科・専攻・課程）>の<氏名（ふりがな）>と申します。

<1–2文: なぜこの会社か — 具体的な製品/求人/技術/事例に触れる>

<2–3文: 自分の接点 — 求人に効く経験だけを、具体的な名詞と数字で>

<入社可能時期・在籍状況（個人ファイルの記載どおり）>
もし可能であれば、カジュアル面談の機会をいただけますと幸いです。履歴書・職務経歴書を添付いたします。

何卒よろしくお願い申し上げます。

<署名: 氏名（ローマ字）/ 所属 / E-mail / TEL>
```

## Cold e-mail (English)

~120–180 words. Subject: `<Role> – <one-line identity> (<start date>)`.
Paragraphs: who I am (one line) → why you, specifically → the 2–3 most relevant things I've done, with
concrete nouns and numbers from the profile → timing → a clear, small ask (a 30-minute chat) → signature with
e-mail and phone.

## Cover letter / 志望動機 / application-form answers

- 志望動機: why this company (specific) → what I bring (evidence) → what I want to build there.
  300–400 字 unless the form says otherwise; respect every form's character limit.
- Map each requirement in the posting to evidence from the profile; for gaps state honestly how the candidate
  is closing them — only if the user confirms it is true.
- Reuse the candidate's self-PR strengths (listed in the personal knowledge file) — don't copy verbatim.

## Replies and follow-ups

- Reply within the thread, same language and politeness level as the company; reply-all when asked.
- Scheduling: check the user's calendars first, then offer 2–3 concrete slots in JST or pick from the
  company's booking page with the user's choice.
- Follow-up after ~7 business days without a reply: two or three sentences, polite, adds one new piece of
  value (e.g. a relevant link) if available.

## Interview prep (interview note)

Logistics (date, format, interviewer) → company recap and role → likely questions with answers in the
interview language (PREP: conclusion first, ~1 minute each; spoken Japanese uses 「御社」) → research
explanation at three depths (30 s / 2 min / technical) → why startup, why now, why this company → visa and
start-date answer → honest answers for gaps → reverse questions with a hypothesis and intent → closing words →
1-minute self-introduction.
